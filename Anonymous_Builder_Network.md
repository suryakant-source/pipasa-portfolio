ANONYMOUSBUILDER NETWORK

Execution System

A selective, identity-blind operating model for high-potential Engineering and Business Management students.

CORE PRINCIPLENo mass community. No follower economy. No status advantage. Entry is selective, identity is hidden, and reputation is earned through real work.

1. Executive System

The network is a private, selective community built around repeated project collaboration. Every project is run as a controlled three-way competition: one challenge, exactly three groups, same deadline, comparable evaluation.

Audience

Qualified students from Engineering and Business Management.

Access

Application → domain assessment → practical task → selection.

Identity

Anonymous to other members; self-reveal is prohibited.

Projects

Three tracks: Engineering-only, Business-only, Mixed Engineering × Business.

Competition

Every project is assigned to exactly 3 groups.

Primary objective

Build a high-trust network through repeated, high-quality collaboration while expanding the founder’s direct network with talent and industry professionals.

2. Non-Negotiable Rules

Selective, not mass-market: quality and commitment are the gate.

Anonymous by default: names, gender, city, college, photo and social handles are hidden from members.

No self-reveal: members cannot disclose identifying information through profiles, chat, files, links or presentations.

Capability-first evaluation: work quality, reasoning, reliability and collaboration matter more than institution or popularity.

Exactly three groups per project: all groups solve the same brief under equivalent constraints.

No follower/like system: status comes from verified contribution, not popularity.

Network through work: repeated collaboration is the main mechanism for trust and relationship formation.

3. Membership & Selection

Initial community target: 30–60 carefully selected members for the pilot, scalable later to 100–300 without changing the operating rules.

Engineering selection  Technical reasoning, practical build task, problem solving, communication and execution discipline.

Business Management selection  Business case, market/strategy reasoning, quantitative thinking, communication and execution discipline.

Final gate  Eligibility check, assessment review, identity verification for administration, acceptance into the anonymous network.

SELECTION LOGICSelect the top candidates for the environment, not the largest number of applicants. The community should feel difficult to enter and worth staying in.

6. Team Formation Engine

Choose participants based on project requirements and maintain domain balance for Mixed projects.

Balance group strength so one group does not receive all top performers.

Minimize repeated pairings using a collaboration-history matrix.

Track availability and avoid overloading the same member across concurrent projects.

Assign anonymous IDs and project roles before members enter the workspace.

NETWORK DESIGN RULEThe algorithm should maximize useful new interactions while keeping the three groups competitively balanced. A member should meet new people across successive projects.

9. Networking Engine

Rotate teammates aggressively enough that members build broad, meaningful relationships across the network.

Use project-based context before connection. A relationship starts with real work, not a cold “connect” button.

Create a Founder/Expert layer: bring founders, CTOs, CMOs, senior engineers and operators into selected challenges as challenge owners, mentors or judges.

Run a weekly/biweekly private Demo Day where the founder of the platform acts as host and connector.

Maintain curated Founder Circle, Expert Circle and Top Builder Circle rather than one giant chat group.

Use introductions selectively: connect people when a real project, skill need or professional opportunity creates a reason to talk.

FOUNDER NETWORK OBJECTIVEThe platform should create valuable relationships for members and simultaneously position the founder as the trusted connector across high-potential students and industry professionals.

12. MVP: First 30 Days

Recruit 30–60 qualified students: roughly balanced across Engineering and Business Management.

Run one Engineering project, one Business Management project and one Mixed project each cycle.

Keep exactly 3 groups per project, ideally 4 members per group in the pilot.

Use a simple private workspace with anonymous IDs, team chat, task board, file submission and evaluation form.

Invite 3–6 external professionals into the first month as challenge owners, mentors or judges.

Measure meaningful relationships created, project completion, team rotation and member quality rather than raw member count.
